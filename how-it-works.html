How it works

From public source
to useful signal.

The important distinction: Markito is not a magic prediction engine. It is a monitoring and filtering system designed to help a human notice relevant public information faster.

1

You define the scope

Choose the assets, companies, sectors and topics you care about. A narrow watchlist is more useful than monitoring everything equally.

2

Public sources are checked

Markito polls supported feeds, pages and APIs for new filings, announcements, headlines and market-data conditions.

3

Changes are normalized

Duplicate stories and repeated updates are grouped so a single event does not become ten separate alerts.

4

Rules score relevance

A filing from a watched company may matter more than a generic headline. Volume deviations can be compared with recent baselines.

5

You get a compact alert

The alert shows what changed, why it was surfaced, when it happened and where the information came from.

6

Results can be reviewed later

Markito can record subsequent market movement so you can evaluate a signal category instead of assuming every alert was useful.

Example

A filing appears.

A useful alert should be understandable in a few seconds, while still letting you verify the source yourself.

What Markito should not do

It should not present “BUY NOW” as if a language model can guarantee an outcome. Market information can inform a decision; it cannot remove uncertainty.
